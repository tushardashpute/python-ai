Sampling & Temperature

`temperature` controls how much randomness the model uses when sampling from its probability distribution.

- Low temperature (0.0–0.2): very deterministic — the model tends to choose the highest-probability tokens. Use for factual, repeatable outputs.
- Medium temperature (0.3–0.6): some variability — balances creativity and reliability.
- High temperature (0.7–1.0): more diverse and creative outputs but higher chance of incorrect or surprising text.

Related knobs:
- `top_k`: keep only the top K most likely tokens before sampling (reduces tail noise).
- `top_p` (nucleus sampling): keep the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability ≥ p (common alternative to `top_k`).

Practical guidance:
- For deterministic or safety-critical outputs (JSON, summaries, code), use `temperature=0` or very low values.
- For creative tasks (brainstorming, ideation), use `temperature=0.7` or higher.
- Combine `temperature` with `top_p` (e.g., `top_p=0.9`) to shape randomness more predictably.

Why hallucination relates: higher randomness can increase plausible-sounding but incorrect continuations. Hallucination is a statistical artifact — reduce sampling variance or add grounding (RAG, retrieval) to mitigate it.
